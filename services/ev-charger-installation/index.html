EV charger installation in Orleans.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Orleans property

Write down the delivery status of the vehicle, the parking spot you expect to use and the distance from that spot to the panel in words. Photograph the wall or post position from the outside.

Ask each contractor which charging details the quote assumes and what changes if a different charger or vehicle is chosen later. Ask also whether a later change of equipment would need a new assessment.

What should the assessment establish?

A circuit planned before the vehicle arrives is built around an assumption, which the scope names along with how the circuit would cope with a different charger later. Where the supply has had a capacity assessment, the scope reports it; where that assessment is excluded, the exclusion is written down instead. The charger location, the cable route and any outdoor-rated components each get a line, and the ESA notification is listed as part of the work. When two quotes for the Orleans installation differ, comparing their stated assumptions shows whether they priced the same design or they rest on different vehicles.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I get a quote before I know which vehicle I am buying?

A contractor can describe a flexible design, but the quote should list its assumptions. Ask what would be reassessed once the vehicle and charger are chosen, and whether that reassessment is already part of the written scope.

Why might two charger quotes describe the circuit differently?

One may assume a particular charger and a short cable run, while another plans for equipment not yet chosen. Compare the stated assumptions, the cable route and what each quote says about outdoor-rated parts.
