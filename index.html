Before work begins

Line up every quote against the same scope.

Two quotes for one job can route cable differently. Where a two-storey home has limited attic or ceiling access, one contractor may fish cable through finished walls and another may open a ceiling; both can work but leave different repairs.

Ask each contractor to write the same items in the same order: what is included, who files the ESA notification, how finished surfaces are restored and what is excluded. A quote silent on one of these is not directly comparable.

What should two written quotes both state before I compare them?

Each should name the work covered, the equipment supplied, the ESA notification, the restoration of finished surfaces and any exclusions. Where one quote is missing an item, ask the contractor to add it in writing before you compare.

Does a shorter quote mean the work is simpler?

Not necessarily. A short quote may leave out restoration, notification or a circuit that another quote includes. Lay the items side by side and ask the contractor about every line that appears in only one quote.

How can two quotes differ when the ceiling cannot be reached from above?

One contractor may cut access openings in finished ceilings, another may route cable through walls. Ask each to state where openings will be made, who repairs and repaints them, and whether that work is in the price.

Who is responsible for the ESA notification named in a quote?

The Licensed Electrical Contractor handles the ESA notification, and a quote should say so. If one quote mentions it and another omits it, ask whether it is included and who handles any related paperwork.

Once I submit the form, who decides which contractor visits?

You choose the contractor. The site is for reading: forms arrive as enquiries and are reviewed, but the site hires nobody, arranges no visits, prices nothing and does no electrical work.
